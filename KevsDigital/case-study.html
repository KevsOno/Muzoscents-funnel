Case study · Large-format supermarket

Finding ₦56 million of at-risk stock before it expired

A supermarket with more than 15,000 active products ran the Kevs bOS expiry report on its live stock. In a single report, the system surfaced every batch nearing expiry, with a combined value of ₦56 million. 98.8% of that value had not yet expired when it was flagged.

The situation

With more than 15,000 active products, nobody can track expiry dates by eye. Tracking expiry at product level, or in a spreadsheet, hides the batch that matters: the delivery that arrived last quarter and will expire before the shelf rotates through it.

What bOS did

  • Tracked expiry per batch, not just per product.
  • Scanned every batch within 150 days of expiry and grouped them into three tiers: expired, critical and high risk.
  • Showed days to expiry and the naira value at risk for each item.
  • Recommended which batch to sell first (FEFO), and kept a timestamped record of each alert and action.

Dark red: already expired. Amber: critical. Light amber: high risk.

Only ₦0.67M of the flagged value had already expired. The other ₦55.3M was still on the shelf and within its date, which is the point of an early warning: the team had time to act instead of writing it off afterwards.

What the team can do with that time

  • Rotate stock so the earliest-expiry batch sells first.
  • Return stock to suppliers where return terms allow.
  • Transfer stock to a location that sells it faster.
  • Mark down the batches that will not sell through in time.

Each action is logged with a timestamp, so there is a record of what was flagged, what was done and when.

Outcome

Within 60 days of running the report, the operational impact went beyond just identifying expired stock:

  • Deeper operational awareness: Staff shifted from reactive checking to proactive management, knowing exactly which batches were nearing expiry.
  • Disciplined shelving: The FEFO recommendations were actively used to rotate stock, ensuring older batches moved to the front.
  • Compliance confidence: The fear of regulatory fines or penalties associated with expired products on the shelf was significantly reduced, backed by the system's timestamped audit trail.
  • More informed procurement: Buying decisions became data-driven, reducing the over-ordering of short-dated stock.
  • Stronger vendor agreements: Armed with precise at-risk inventory data, management was able to negotiate better return terms and delivery schedules with suppliers.

What this case study does and does not show

It shows that bOS can find expiry risk early across a very large catalogue, and put a naira value on it.

It does not claim that ₦56 million was saved. Flagged value is stock at risk, not money recovered, and this is one report from one business.
